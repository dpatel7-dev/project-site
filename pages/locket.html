locket

One password to remember.

locket keeps all your passwords locked up with one master password (or Touch ID), and makes them easy to use: copy one with a click, make strong new ones, see 2-step codes, and find out which ones are weak, used twice, or leaked. Accounts sorts them by the email you signed up with, and locket in Chrome fills them in on websites and offers to save new ones when you sign in or sign up. Bastion the armadillo walks about the desert while it's unlocked, and curls up into a ball when it locks.

How your passwords are kept safe

  • Your master password goes through scrypt (slow on purpose, so guessing takes a very long time), which unlocks a random key that locks your passwords with AES-256-GCM. Your master password is never saved anywhere.
  • If you forget your master password, nobody can get your passwords back, not even locket. Write it down and keep it somewhere safe.
  • Touch ID is Apple's own check, so a photo or a video can't fool it. (Mac only.)
  • The leak check (Have I Been Pwned) never sends your passwords: only the first 5 characters of each one's fingerprint leave your computer.
  • locket in Chrome only talks to locket on your own computer, after you pair it with a 6-digit code. It only fills in on secure (https) websites, and only on the website a password was saved for, so a look-alike website gets nothing.
  • Copied passwords are marked secret, so clipboard keepers like magpie skip them.
  • Your vault stays on your computer, in ~/.locket. Works on Mac, Windows and Linux.
