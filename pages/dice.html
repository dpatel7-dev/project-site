Put it on your computer

DiceOS runs on a Raspberry Pi or on a PC. Writing DiceOS erases whatever is on that card, drive or USB stick, so use a different one from Home Assistant's.

  1. Download DiceOSOne file, about a gigabyte: dice-arm64.img.xz.
    Download DiceOS
  2. Open Raspberry Pi ImagerGet it free from raspberrypi.com/software. On a Mac it asks for an administrator password to write cards.
  3. Choose your Pi, then the DiceOS fileUnder Operating System, scroll to the bottom, choose Use custom, and pick the file you downloaded.
  4. Choose where it goesThe microSD card, or the NVMe drive in a USB adapter. When it asks about OS customisation, choose No: your dice.json does that.
  5. Write, then add your settingsWhen it's finished, the card shows up as a drive called bootfs. Drag your dice.json onto it (and dice-files.zip, if you made one), then eject it. bootfs has room for about 400 MB of files; for more, put both on a USB stick instead.
  6. Switch the Pi onThe die turns while it starts, then DiceOS says hello by name.

NVMe drives

On a Pi 5 with an NVMe drive, write DiceOS onto the drive through a USB adapter, or use the Pi itself (the next tab). With no microSD card in, the Pi 5 starts from the NVMe drive.

  1. Copy two files onto a USB stickThe DiceOS file and your dice.json (and dice-files.zip, if you made one). Copying files doesn't need an administrator.
    Download DiceOS
  2. Plug everything into the PiThe USB stick, a keyboard, a screen, an Ethernet cable to your router, and the microSD card or NVMe drive for DiceOS. Take Home Assistant's card out first.
  3. Start Network InstallSwitch the Pi on. With nothing to start from, it offers Network Install: hold Shift. It downloads Raspberry Pi Imager and opens it right on the Pi.
  4. Choose the DiceOS fileUnder Operating System, choose Use custom, and pick the DiceOS file on the USB stick.
  5. Choose where it goes, and writeThe microSD card or the NVMe drive. Then Write.
  6. Restart with the USB stick still inDiceOS finds dice.json on the stick and sets everything up.

If Use custom isn't there

Pick Raspberry Pi OS Lite (64-bit) instead and set your Wi-Fi in Imager's settings. Then follow the last tab with your setup code.

  1. Get a setup codePress Get a setup code above. It holds everything except your passwords.
  2. Paste one line into Terminal on the PiIt installs DiceOS and takes your settings.
    curl -fsSL {{origin}}/dice/install.sh | sudo bash
  3. RestartType sudo reboot. To take DiceOS off later: sudo /opt/dice/bin/dice-uninstall

What it changes

It adds DiceOS in /opt/dice and starts it instead of the usual desktop. Your files stay where they are.

  1. Download DiceOS for PCsOne file, about a gigabyte and a half: dice-amd64.iso.
    Download DiceOS for PCs
  2. Write it onto a USB stick8 GB or bigger. In Raspberry Pi Imager choose Use custom and pick the file, or use balenaEtcher. Everything on the stick is erased.
  3. Start the PC from the stickPlug it in and switch the PC on. Tap its start-up menu key straight away (often F12, F11, F9 or Esc) and pick the USB stick.
  4. Try it, or install itPick DiceOS to try it from the stick. Pick Install to put it on the PC's own drive: that erases the drive, and asks for your name and a password.
  5. Bring your settingsPut your dice.json (and dice-files.zip, if you made one) on a second USB stick and plug it in too. DiceOS finds it when it starts.

If the PC won't start from the stick

Look in its settings (often F2 or Del as it starts) for the start-up order, and put USB first. Some PCs also need Secure Boot turned off.

  1. Get a setup codePress Get a setup code above. It holds everything except your passwords.
  2. Paste one line into Terminal on the PCIt installs DiceOS and takes your settings. Debian 12 or newer, or Ubuntu 24.04 or newer, 64-bit.
    curl -fsSL {{origin}}/dice/install.sh | sudo bash
  3. RestartType sudo reboot. To take DiceOS off later: sudo /opt/dice/bin/dice-uninstall

Good to know

Which computers
A Raspberry Pi 5 or 4 with a screen (the official touch screens work, and can be turned in Settings), or a 64-bit PC that can start from a USB stick.
Home Assistant
Keep it on its own card or drive and it stays exactly as it is. Put its address above for a dashboard button.
The password
The user is dice, password dice, until you set one above or in Settings, You.
Your passwords
They never leave this computer. After the first start, DiceOS wipes them out of dice.json and renames it dice-applied.json.
Trying it first
The emulator starts DiceOS with your settings on a pretend Pi or PC, right here in the browser, and shows what it did with them.
Changing things later
In DiceOS, Settings, This computer: the computer's name, time zone, keyboard, SSH and the rest, or Use a dice.json to take a whole new one. It can save its own as dice.json too, to set up another computer the same way.
Switching to your other things
DiceOS can switch the TV or your computer screen to a PlayStation, a cable box or a laptop, and share its keyboard and mouse: Inputs, step by step shows how, with pictures.
What does Dice do that other computers don't?
Oops undoes anything you changed, not just typing: a setting, a closed window, a file in the Trash (Super + Z, or "Deuce, undo that"). Lookouts keep watch for you: "tell me when the Knicks game starts", "let me know when it stops raining", "tell me when this page changes". And Show me how: ask "how do I pair headphones?" and Dice opens the right place and lights up the very button to press.
Deuce's brain
After installing, Settings, Deuce, Set it up gives Deuce a language model that runs on the computer itself, picked for it (Smart, about 3.4 GB, on a Raspberry Pi 5 with 8 GB; bigger and smarter on a PC). Then Deuce understands whatever you say, keeps up with a conversation, does several things at once, answers questions from the web with its sources and writes things for you. Nothing you say leaves the computer.
Traced mode
Turn it on above (or later in Settings, Look) and your world is painted by hand, pixel by pixel, with an ink line round everything and small things going on if you watch: sheep grazing, a train crossing the desert, a frog hopping between lily pads. The desktop's glass gets finer edges, and DiceOS starts up by drawing your world in front of you.
Ads
Shield blocks ads, pop-ups and trackers in every website and every app on DiceOS, YouTube's ads included. It's on from the start; Settings, Shield changes it.
Just curious?
DiceWeb is the same thing in your browser: the desktop, Music, Notes, Files and a pretend Terminal.
